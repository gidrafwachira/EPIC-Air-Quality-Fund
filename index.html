My onboarding and continuous support playbook

How I take an awardee from signed agreement to final impact story, and what each step achieves. The two steps marked as proposals are additions I'd suggest for the EPIC Air Quality Fund cohort.

  1. Grant agreement drafting, review and signing

    Achieves: clear, shared expectations before any money moves.

    • Proposed work plan with clear milestones. I prefer milestone-based tranche payments to time-based ones.
    • Primary and secondary project contacts documented.
    • Reporting frequency documented and reporting template shared.
    • Data and data-sharing requirements documented, with any other requirements of the fund.

  2. Kick-off meeting

    Achieves: an agreed work plan and calendar that I can track from day one.

    • Discuss the fund's expectations and requirements.
    • Agree a high-level work plan with milestones. This is the plan I track in the project management tool, such as Asana.
    • Agree reporting and invoicing timelines, also tracked in the tool.
    • Meet the primary and secondary contacts.
    • Schedule quarterly check-ins, or whatever rhythm is feasible for the team.
    • Share the risk and lessons-learned matrix template. The team records positive and negative lessons, and the matrix becomes part of every check-in agenda.

  3. Cohort orientation sessionProposed for EPIC

    Achieves: one cohort that knows each other and hears the same rules once.

    • Held just after all awardees have signed their grant agreements.
    • Cohort members get to know each other.
    • Fund-wide requirements and expectations are shared with everyone at once.

  4. Quarterly check-ins

    Achieves: problems surface early, in an informal conversation rather than a late report.

    • Review progress against the work plan.
    • Review risks and lessons learned, and act on any recommendations.
    • Review or collect data for M&E, communications and reporting.

  5. Semi-annual progress info sessionProposed for EPIC

    Achieves: awardees learn from each other, not only from the Fund.

    • Awardees share learnings and challenges.
    • Peers exchange ideas across countries.

  6. Interim reports

    Achieves: payments tied to verified progress.

    • Review for accuracy and completeness.
    • Request the invoice once the report is approved.

  7. Final report

    Achieves: a clean close-out, and results that travel beyond the grant.

    • Review for accuracy and completeness.
    • Request the final invoice after the report is approved.
    • Awardee presents their impact at a working group, community of practice, conference or summit.
    • Write the impact story with the awardee and the communications and M&E teams.

Your grantmaking principles, and how I've worked

Four of the Fund's published principles, each next to how I've run grants in practice.

Fund in-country ownership and global collaboration

Awardees are in-country actors who own their data, and that data feeds global systems.

My portfolios funded community-based organisations directly, then connected them through working groups, learning forums and global summits.

Fund flexibly

No caps on operating costs, and budgets that can move between lines as needs change.

I ran flexible grants and used no-cost extensions to keep projects on track when reality changed, instead of penalising awardees for it.

Fund for the long term

18-month awards, renewable up to twice.

Renewals are where good records matter most. I'd make sure every renewal brief draws on 18 months of clean tracker data.

Fund where others don't yet

Capable local actors in countries that receive little clean-air funding.

My whole career has been with local organisations in places donors overlook, from Tana River County to Yumbe District.

    How I use AI, and where I stop it

    You asked for comfort using AI appropriately and ethically to streamline repetitive work. That means it handles first drafts and checking, while people make every judgement.

    TaskAI doesI doNever handed to AI
    Report completeness checks Compares a narrative report to the reporting template and lists missing or thin sections. Read the report myself, confirm each gap, write the follow-up. Any view on awardee performance or standing.
    Awardee correspondence Drafts from the response library; first-pass French, Spanish or Portuguese for awardees who prefer them. Edit tone and facts for each awardee; have important translations checked by a fluent speaker. Sending anything without a human read.
    Background research Summarises public information on an organisation, with links to sources. Check every fact against its source before it enters a brief. Unverified claims in renewal or onboarding material.
    Tracker and data cleaning Writes Excel formulas; flags duplicates, impossible dates and outliers. Spot-check results and fix the source data. Invoice approval or payment decisions.
    Newsletter and impact summaries Condenses awardee updates into short AirWatch drafts. Confirm wording with the awardee before publication. Quotes or results the awardee hasn't approved.

    Ground rules

    Use only the AI tools the University of Chicago approves, under its data policies.

    Keep banking details, personal data and confidential financials out of AI tools that aren't approved for them.

    A person makes every determination on payments, performance and renewals.

    Say when AI helped, if anyone asks.
